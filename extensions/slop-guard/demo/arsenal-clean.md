# Four wins, one goal conceded: Arsenal's start is real, and October will tell us how real

Arsenal top the Premier League on 12 points from 12, level with Manchester City but ahead on goal difference, and the number that should worry their rivals isn't the eight goals scored. It's the one conceded. Four league games, one goal against. That's not a hot streak; that's a base you can build a title challenge on, because it survives the days when the finishing goes cold.

What we know so far: a 1-0 win over Aston Villa — the kind of tight, patient result that used to be Arsenal's weakness — and three more league wins to make it four from four. Then on Wednesday they went to Ipswich and won 4-2 in the League Cup, rotating the squad and still scoring four. Rotation that doesn't dilute the attack is a luxury this club hasn't had in a decade.

Now the honest part: the fixture list has been kind, and the calendar is about to stop being kind. Brighton away on Saturday is a live test — they're fifth, they've scored 13 in four games, and the Amex is where optimism goes to get audited. After the international break: Leeds at home, Lille at home in Europe, Forest away, and then the stretch that decides the narrative — Bayern away in the Champions League on October 21, Everton at home, and Liverpool at Anfield on November 1.

That's the window. If Arsenal are still top of the league on the morning of November 2, the conversation stops being "nice start" and becomes "can anyone catch them." If they take, say, four points from Brighton, Liverpool and the Bayern trip combined, they'll still be in the race — the difference is nobody will believe it, including possibly them.

My own read, for the record: the defence is the signal, the attack is the noise. Eight goals in four gets the highlights; one conceded in four wins titles. The test at Brighton is whether the back line holds against a team that has already scored 13 times this season. If it does, circle November 1.

One claim you can hold me to: Arsenal will not be out of the top two by the end of October. If they are, this start was fixture-list mirage — and I'll say so.
